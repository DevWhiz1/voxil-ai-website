Chatbots went from a frustrating widget in the corner of a website to one of the most-used software interfaces in the world. This page collects 45+ chatbot statistics for 2026, and every figure links to the organization that published it: survey firms, analysts and the companies themselves, not other roundups. We note where a number is a forecast or a vendor’s own claim.

Key chatbot statistics at a glance

  • 49% of U.S. adults use AI chatbots, up from about a third in 2024 (Pew Research Center, Americans and AI 2026, 2026).
  • 24% of U.S. adults use AI chatbots every day (Pew Research Center, Americans and AI 2026, 2026).
  • 800 million people used ChatGPT every week as of October 2025 (OpenAI (DevDay keynote), 2025).
  • 85% of customer service organizations use at least one form of AI (Salesforce, State of Service: AI Agents Edition, 2026).
  • 66% of service organizations use agentic AI, up from 39% a year earlier (Salesforce, State of Service: AI Agents Edition, 2026).
  • 50% of service cases are expected to be handled by AI by 2027 (Salesforce, State of Service (7th edition), 2025).
  • 74% of consumers expect 24/7 service because of AI (Zendesk, CX Trends 2026, 2025).
  • 64% of customers would prefer companies didn’t use AI for customer service (Gartner customer survey, 2024).
  • $49.9 billion projected conversational AI market by 2030 (one analyst estimate) (MarketsandMarkets, Conversational AI Market, 2024).

Chatbot usage statistics

ChatGPT is still the most-used chatbot, but it is no longer alone: Google says the Gemini app passed one billion monthly users (Alphabet Q3 2026 earnings, CEO remarks, 2026), and Meta reports the same milestone for Meta AI (Meta Q3 2025 earnings call, 2025). Stanford’s AI Index notes generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years, faster than the PC or the internet (Stanford HAI, AI Index Report, 2026).

Mass consumer use matters for businesses because it changes expectations. People who ask an AI assistant questions every day increasingly expect a website or WhatsApp line to answer in plain language, instantly, not to present a menu of buttons or a contact form.

How many companies use chatbots?

There is no single global count of companies running chatbots, but every major survey points the same way. McKinsey finds 78% of organizations use AI in at least one business function (McKinsey, The State of AI, 2025), and Salesforce finds 85% of customer service organizations use some form of AI (Salesforce, State of Service: AI Agents Edition, 2026). Small businesses are catching up: the U.S. Chamber of Commerce reported 40% using generative AI in 2024, nearly double the year before (U.S. Chamber of Commerce, Empowering Small Business, 2024).

Adoption is often informal, though. Microsoft and LinkedIn found most AI users at work bring their own tools (Microsoft & LinkedIn, Work Trend Index, 2024). For customer-facing chatbots that’s a problem: a bot talking to your customers needs approved content, guardrails and integration with your CRM, not an employee’s personal subscription.

Chatbots in customer service

Service teams expect AI to handle half of all cases by 2027, up from 30% today (Salesforce, State of Service (7th edition), 2025), and 70% of organizations using AI service agents report measurable value within 60 days (Salesforce, State of Service: AI Agents Edition, 2026). Klarna’s assistant, which handled two-thirds of service chats in its first month (Klarna press release, 2024), became the headline example. The company later said it would keep human agents available alongside AI, which is the more useful lesson: automation works best as the fast lane, not the only lane.

Chatbot forecasts and market size

Chatbots are part of the wider conversational AI market, which also includes voice assistants and AI agents. Market-size estimates vary with how each analyst defines the market, so treat any single figure as one forecast rather than a fact. One estimate projects the conversational AI market at about $49.9 billion by 2030, up from roughly $13.2 billion in 2024 (MarketsandMarkets, Conversational AI Market, 2024); another projects the AI agents market at $52.6 billion by 2030 (MarketsandMarkets, AI Agents Market (press release), 2025). The direction of travel matters more than the exact number.

What customers think about chatbots

What these numbers say together
Customers want instant, around-the-clock answers, but they don’t want to be trapped. 45% are more likely to use an AI agent when there’s a clear path to a human, and three in four want to know when they’re talking to AI. Bots that disclose themselves, remember context and hand off cleanly avoid most of the complaints behind the 64%.

Vendor-reported results show what well-built LLM agents can reach: Intercom says its Fin agent averages a 76% resolution rate across more than 12,000 customers (Intercom, Fin (vendor-reported), 2026) and resolves about 2 million conversations a week (Intercom, Fin (vendor-reported), 2026). These are the vendor’s own figures, so treat them as a ceiling rather than a typical result.

The ROI reality check

Research from MIT’s NANDA initiative found most organizations saw no measurable P&L return from generative-AI pilots (MIT NANDA, The GenAI Divide, 2025), and Forrester predicts a third of companies will harm customer experiences with frustrating AI self-service in 2026 (Forrester, Predictions 2026, 2025). The pattern behind the successes: a specific workflow, integration with real systems, and learning from real conversations. A chatbot that books appointments into your calendar and writes leads into your CRM has a measurable outcome. A chatbot that "answers questions" often doesn’t.

What to measure if you deploy a chatbot

  • Resolution rate, conversations where the customer got what they needed without a human.
  • Lead capture rate, share of conversations that produce a qualified contact.
  • Bookings, appointments or calls booked directly by the bot.
  • Escalation quality, did the human receive the full context?
  • After-hours share, how much value arrives when your team is offline.

These statistics are context, not a business case. The useful numbers are your own: how many questions your team answers each week, how many inquiries arrive after hours and how many leads wait too long for a reply.
