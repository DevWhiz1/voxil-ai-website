Switching from GoHighLevel: How to Migrate Out Without Losing Data

Leaving GoHighLevel is mostly an export-and-rebuild job. Here’s what comes out cleanly, what has to be rebuilt by hand, and the order that avoids losing leads mid-move.

Key takeaways
  • Contacts, tags, custom fields and opportunities export to CSV; workflows, funnels, calendars and AI settings do not.
  • There is no simple bulk export of conversation history, so plan how you will keep it.
  • Phone numbers and A2P registrations need planning early, because texting can stop if they are moved carelessly.
  • Run both systems in parallel for a short period and only cancel once leads flow into the new CRM.
  • Before leaving, check whether a rebuild inside GoHighLevel would fix the real problem.

GoHighLevel does a lot in one place, which is exactly why leaving it feels risky. Your CRM, pipelines, calendars, phone numbers, texting, funnels and automations may all live in one account. Moving to another CRM is very doable, but it is an export-and-rebuild project, not a one-click transfer. This guide shows what moves cleanly, what doesn’t and the order that keeps leads flowing.

First: is switching the right fix?

Many teams that want to leave GoHighLevel are really frustrated with a messy setup: duplicate pipelines, broken workflows, too many tools nobody uses. Ask whether the problem is the platform or the build.

Phone numbers and texting

Numbers are where migrations go wrong. If customers know your number, you’ll want to port it to the new provider rather than replace it. Ask GoHighLevel support and your new provider about the porting process and timing before you start.

US business texting also needs A2P 10DLC registration on the new provider. Registration can take time, so start it early and keep texting on the old system until the new registration is approved.

A migration order that doesn’t lose leads

  1. Audit: list pipelines, custom fields, workflows, forms, calendars, numbers, integrations and AI agents in use.
  2. Set up the new CRM: fields, pipeline stages, users and permissions before any import.
  3. Export and clean data: deduplicate contacts, fix formats and map every field.
  4. Import: contacts first, then opportunities, then any notes or history you’re keeping.
  5. Rebuild automations: lead routing, follow-up, reminders and review requests, the ones that bring in revenue first.
  6. Reconnect lead sources: website forms, ads, chat widgets and phone numbers.
  7. Run in parallel: for one to two weeks, confirm every new lead lands in the new CRM.
  8. Cancel last: once you’re sure nothing still depends on GoHighLevel, and after confirming how long your data stays available.

Where teams usually go

  • HubSpot: for sales teams that want deeper reporting and a large app ecosystem.
  • Salesforce or Microsoft Dynamics: for larger companies with complex processes.
  • Industry tools such as practice management or field service software with built-in CRM.
  • A lighter CRM plus automation using n8n, Make or Zapier for the workflows.
