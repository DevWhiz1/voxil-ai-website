Clients are asking their agencies for AI receptionists and calling agents. Most agencies don’t want to hire voice engineers to build them. White label AI voice agents solve that: a partner provides the technology, and you sell, brand and own the client relationship. This guide covers how it works, the three main routes, real cost ranges and how to price it.

What “white label” means for voice AI

A white label AI voice agent is an AI phone agent that answers or makes calls in your client’s name, built or hosted by a third party, and sold by your agency as your own product. The client sees your brand on the proposal, dashboard and invoice. The partner stays invisible.

The same model covers white label AI receptionists (inbound answering and booking), AI calling agents (speed-to-lead, reminders, reactivation) and voice agents connected to a CRM.

1. White label voice AI platforms

These wrap a voice engine (often Vapi or Retell AI) in a dashboard you can rebrand with your logo and domain. You create agents from templates, connect phone numbers and bill clients yourself. They are quick to start, but the agents are only as good as your prompt and call design, and your clients can often find the same platform themselves.

2. GoHighLevel Voice AI with rebilling

If your clients already live in GoHighLevel, its Voice AI answers calls inside each sub-account with native access to contacts, calendars and workflows. Agencies on the $497 plan can rebill AI usage with a markup. See our GoHighLevel AI Employee pricing guide for current plans and per-minute rates.

3. Custom agents built by a partner

A specialist builds each agent for the client’s actual calls: conversation design, voice and latency tuning, booking logic, CRM and phone integration, and testing on real calls. You sell it as your own. This is what we do in our white label AI agents program.

How to price white label voice AI

  • Sell outcomes, not minutes. Clients care about calls answered and appointments booked. Package as a monthly fee per location with an included call volume.
  • Charge setup. Every good agent needs discovery, call design and testing. A setup fee covers it and filters out unserious clients.
  • Keep usage rebillable. Run voice and phone costs on an account you can pass through with a margin, so heavy users don’t eat your profit.
  • Add a usage tier. Include a sensible minute allowance and charge for overage, rather than “unlimited”.
Margin check
Before quoting, multiply the client’s expected monthly call minutes by your all-in per-minute cost (voice + telephony), add your support time, then set the retainer at a margin you’d be happy with even in a busy month.

What to look for in a white label partner

  1. Brand control: your name everywhere the client looks, no partner branding.
  2. Ownership: agents, prompts and accounts in your name (or your client’s), so you can move if needed.
  3. Integrations: real booking into the client’s calendar and CRM, including GoHighLevel.
  4. Testing and tuning: a supervised pilot on real calls, then ongoing improvements.
  5. Compliance: consent for outbound calls, AI disclosure and recording rules. Read our TCPA guide for AI calling before selling outbound.
  6. Support model: who fixes what, how fast, and how issues reach the partner without your client seeing them.

Retell, Vapi or GoHighLevel?

Retell AI is the more packaged option, with built-in telephony and a visual builder. Vapi gives developers more control over every component. GoHighLevel Voice AI wins when the client’s CRM is already GHL. Our Vapi vs Retell AI comparison goes deeper, and we build white label agents on all three.
