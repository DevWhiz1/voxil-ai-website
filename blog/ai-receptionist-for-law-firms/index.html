AI Receptionist for Law Firms: Legal Intake, Screening and Consultation Booking

Potential clients call once, often after hours and often in a hurry. Here’s how an AI receptionist answers, screens and books consultations for law firms, without giving legal advice.

Abdul Moeez · AI Automation Expert Published November 8, 2026 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • An AI receptionist answers every call, captures the matter details and books a consultation with the right attorney.
  • It screens by practice area, location and case type using rules your firm sets.
  • It collects names of opposing parties for your conflict check before anything confidential is discussed.
  • It never gives legal advice and says clearly that no attorney-client relationship has been formed.
  • After-hours and overflow calls are the fastest win, especially for personal injury, family and criminal defence.

For many law firms, the first phone call decides whether a case comes to you or to the firm down the street. Potential clients call after an accident, an arrest or a family crisis, often outside office hours, and they keep calling until someone picks up. An AI receptionist answers every call, gathers the facts your intake team needs and books the consultation, while leaving legal judgement to your attorneys.

What an AI receptionist does for a law firm

  1. Answers every call in your firm’s name, 24/7, including overflow when staff are busy.
  2. Identifies the matter type: personal injury, family, criminal, immigration, estate planning, employment and so on.
  3. Screens against your criteria: jurisdiction, statute-of-limitations flags, case type and any minimums you set.
  4. Collects conflict-check details: the caller’s name and the names of opposing parties, before any confidential facts.
  5. Books a consultation with the right attorney or intake specialist.
  6. Sends a confirmation with what to bring, and logs the full intake in your case management system or CRM.

Why it matters for intake

Calls that go unanswered are common for small businesses (411 Locals small-business phone study, 2016), and speed decides who wins the client: responding within an hour makes a lead far more likely to qualify (Harvard Business Review, “The Short Life of Online Sales Leads”, 2011). Clients also expect to reach someone at any hour; 74% of consumers now expect 24/7 service because of AI (Zendesk, CX Trends 2026, 2025).

Ethics and confidentiality

Guardrails every firm should set
The AI must not give legal advice, predict outcomes or quote fees beyond what you approve. It should tell callers it is an AI assistant, that the call does not create an attorney-client relationship, and avoid collecting detailed confidential facts until your conflict check is cleared.
  • Supervision: treat the AI like non-lawyer staff. An attorney should approve its scripts and review its output.
  • Confidentiality: choose vendors with business terms that exclude training on your data, encrypt recordings and transcripts, and restrict access.
  • Disclosure: nearly three in four consumers want to know when they’re talking to AI (Salesforce, State of the Connected Customer, 2024).
  • Local rules: check your state bar’s guidance on AI, advertising and solicitation, and recording-consent laws for calls.

This is general information, not legal or ethics advice for your jurisdiction.

Connecting to your case management system

The best setups write each intake straight into your case management system or CRM, with the matter type, contact details, conflict-check names, call summary and recording. AI receptionist vs legal answering service

Traditional legal answering services take messages; a well-built AI receptionist can screen, book and update your systems instantly, at a lower cost per call. Many firms combine the two, with AI handling routine intake and people taking sensitive calls. Our AI receptionist vs answering service comparison goes into the trade-offs.

Rollout plan

  1. Define intake rules: practice areas, jurisdictions, disqualifiers, conflict-check fields and routing.
  2. Write and approve scripts with an attorney, including disclaimers and escalation triggers.
  3. Test with realistic calls, including distressed callers and out-of-scope matters.
  4. Launch after hours and on overflow, with intake staff reviewing every record.
  5. Expand once screening and booking accuracy are proven.
